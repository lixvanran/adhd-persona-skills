- **[O] The core argument, verbatim:**

### 3.3 The current field position — and Hallowell's absence from it
- **[O]** *Nature*, 26 Nov 2025, Helen Pearson, "ADHD diagnoses are growing. What's going on?", doi:10.1038/d41586-025-03855-2.
  - US: **>11% of children ever diagnosed** (2024 CDC report) vs 8% in 2003.
  - **RFK / MAHA commission** stated ADHD is part of "overdiagnosis and overtreatment" and that ADHD drugs are **"not beneficial to children in the long run."**
  - Two global studies put **true** prevalence at **~5.4% children / ~2.6% adults** — i.e., "true" prevalence is stable worldwide while diagnosed prevalence climbs.
  - Cortese et al. meta-analysis: **19% of ADHD school-age children take medication, vs ~70% who would benefit** — i.e., evidence of *under*-treatment as well as over-diagnosis.
  - Max Wiznitzer: *"The prescribing rate is rising because we are better at finding children who have problems."* And he disputes the MAHA report.
  - Stephen Hinshaw: some clinicians diagnose in *"15–20 minutes."*
  - Sibley's **U-curve** finding: too little OR too much stress/responsibility impairs performance.
- **[I] Hallowell is not interviewed in this piece.** No evidence found that he has responded to the RFK/MAHA position. **Information insufficient** — but the absence is itself notable, since he is the field's most publicly available popularizer and would be the obvious person for *Nature* to call.

Rohde, who **participated in the revision**, said: *"So when we expand the criteria, obviously you increase a little bit the prevalence."* Hallowell **supports** the DSM-5 definition. ## 5. Criticism of the "ADHD Is an Advantage" Framework

### 5.1 Barkley — the named, sustained, and specific critique
- **[O]** **Barkley on ADHD as a gift (a widely circulated post):**
  > *"Many people may be gifted and talented in various aspects of these other human abilities, but never attribute that giftedness or that success to the AD/HD itself… I want people to understand that while people might be gifted and talented and successful in spite of their AD/HD, it is not because of their AD/HD. The AD/HD itself may, in fact, make you less effective than other people who have equally talented areas in those human abilities."*
- **[O]** **Barkley on the advocacy consequences:**
  > *"It's going to be very hard for society to take you all seriously if you continue to trumpet this disorder as a gift. There is no way that we can go to Ottawa and walk the halls of Parliament arguing for accommodations, entitlements, funding of AD/HD medications on the one hand, while rah-rah cheering AD/HD as this wonderful giftedness that we have and you don't."*
  Source: https://psychotherapysphere.com/tag/adhd-is-no-gift/
- **[O]** **Barkley on Hallowell specifically** (CHADD 2011): *"Hallowell likes to point to several famous examples of individuals who have started successful copier businesses or airline enterprises to assert the giftedness of those with ADHD… Their ADHD may have helped them to some extent, but in the context of those around them."* He adds that **supportive parents, coaches, and administrative assistants** were decisive, and: *"In Barkley's research into groups of people… we come up with a portrait of averages, not a composite of any one person."*
  Source: https://doczz.net/doc/2216048/when-dr.-barkley-met-dr.-hallowell
- **[O]** Library Journal review of Barkley's *12 Principles for Raising a Child with ADHD* (2020): **"Barkley disagrees with experts who label ADHD a 'gift,' contending that ADHD cannot both be a gift and a serious disability that requires services."** Note the argument is **logical, not empirical** — a genuine gift/disability contradiction.
  Source: https://www.libraryjournal.com/review/12-principles-for-raising-a-child-with-adhd
- **[I] Barkley's specific charge against Hallowell's method:** anecdote-over-data. | Axis | Hallowell | Maté |
|---|---|---|
| Primary cause | Neurodevelopmental; heritable; "inborn genetics" (Vermont test) | Early relational stress / emotional absence |
| Genetic evidence | Cited as settling the matter | Dismissed as "a sensitive child" effect |
| Medication | Central, "incredibly and unbelievably safe" | De-emphasized |
| Trauma | Addressed as a personal biographical fact (his own family) | The causal engine |

  Source: https://orionsmethod.com/podcast/adhd-as-a-superpower-with-dr-ned-hallowell/
- **[I] So Hallowell's position is: "trauma shaped my *life*, but not my *brain*." Maté's is: "trauma shaped my *brain*."** Both accept that the life stories rhyme.
