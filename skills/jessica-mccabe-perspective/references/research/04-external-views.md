## 2. The one named clinical rebuttal — Jonathan Murphy, PMHNP-BC

**The most direct professional objection located.**

`[THIRD PARTY]` **Jonathan Murphy, PMHNP-BC** — board-certified Psychiatric Mental Health Nurse Practitioner (board certified since 2017; licensed MA, NH, OR, WA; NPI 1821441965; ANCC Board Certified; CHADD and ADDitude professional member; LegitScript certified), operates the channel **My Focus Path**. He published a video titled **"This Piece of Advice Is Most Certainly NOT 'How to ADHD' | Debunking Jessica McCabe"**.

**His argument, verbatim from the video description:**
> "**ADHD influencer Jessica McCabe claims consistency isn't possible with ADHD.** Jonathan Murphy, PMHNP-BC, a Board-Certified Psychiatric Mental Health Nurse Practitioner, disagrees — and brings the science to prove it. … **The 'woe is me wormhole' is real. Learned helplessness is real. And content that tells you consistency isn't possible may be confirming a bias that keeps you stuck. Consistency IS possible with ADHD.**"

**The specific target:** a How to ADHD video (title begins *"5 Lies I Believed About Productivity (As S…"*) and a timestamp within it.

**His evidence:** cites B.F. Skinner (operant conditioning), Edward Bernays, BJ Fogg (Behaviour Model), Nir Eyal (*Hooked*), and *ADHD 2.0* by Hallowell & Ratey (oscillation model). His method: **"Make it SMALL. Not 'write for 30 minutes' — write ONE SENTENCE. Not 'go to the gym for two hours' — PUT ON GYM SHOES."** His personal proof: 50 videos since 12 September 2024, the first 30 in 30 consecutive days — *"I have ADHD. I've slayed my dragons."*

**His framing of the professional norm — ⚠️ the most pointed line in the entire corpus, and it is aimed squarely at her:**
> "**Russell Barkley engages with YouTube ADHD content regularly. This is what professionals do — we discuss ideas, challenge claims, and advance the conversation. This is clinical discourse.**"
> Source: http://youpak.com/watch?v=VgTrpGvhpV0 (Clipzag mirror of the video description)

⚠️ `[INFERENCE]` **This is a Barkley-adjacent critique.** Murphy cites Barkley's *ADHD 2.0*, invokes Barkley as the model of professional engagement with exactly this kind of content, and targets the specific claim she is most associated with. Whether or not Barkley knows, endorses, or agrees with Murphy is **unknown and unverifiable** — Barkley's YouTube channel is full of rebuttals to ADHD creators and Murphy explicitly frames it as citing him.

⚠️ **Note the courteous framing.** Murphy opens by praising her: *"Jessica McCabe and How to ADHD have done incredible work for ADHD awareness. This video addresses one specific clinical point."* `[INFERENCE]` This is **not** the "she's dangerous" register of influencer criticism. It is a narrow, technical, single-claim dispute about whether behavioural consistency is achievable — the kind of disagreement two professionals have.

⚠️ **`[INFERENCE]` The single sharpest critique of her in the entire corpus, reduced to its core:** she is accused not of being wrong about ADHD but of **describing an incapacity as an identity in a way that forecloses the modification of that behaviour.** "Learned helplessness" is the technical term, and it lands hard, because her most repeated line — *"no matter how many tools you have, the challenges are still there"* — is read by Murphy as *learned helplessness* rather than as honest realism. **Her signature strength is her signature liability.** See file 03 §8.2.

⚠️ **`[INFERENCE]` No response from McCabe, no public rebuttal, and no third-party discussion of this exchange was found.** Asymmetric: the professional critique exists; the reply does not.

## 6. Criticism from within the ADHD community

⚠️ **`[INFERENCE]` This is the category where the evidence is thinnest and my search least successful.** Reddit was not directly accessible to me. I found **no** systematic community backlash. What I did find:

### 6.1 Reader criticism of the book — the most substantive criticism available
All from https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/125227576-how-to-adhd

**The most damaging review on record (Feb 2024), verbatim:**
> "How to put it… this is exactly how one imagines a book written by a YouTuber: **superficial but with glitter on top**, so that it appears more exciting. The famous research that apparently went into it is **repeating easily available and well known facts that have already been written many times; the tools consist of lists of 'do this' with a careful omission of the 'how.'** Another – and more serious – issue is that despite claims, **Jessica McCabe's knowledge on ADHD is very limited and covers only what she is experiencing.** She does acknowledge that this condition is a spectrum and that not everything works for everybody, but **one really needs to be a certain shape and size (and have a six-figure income from Patreon) for them to be effective.** The chapter about environmental impact and contribution is not enough to cover this gaping black hole. **In short, this book is a product of our times, when everything is a hustle and everything can be turned into an income if you have enough charisma to become an influencer.**"

**Second review:** *"the book is about 150 pages too long and her **'Teehee I'm ADHD and quirky' shtick gets old pretty quickly**."*

**Third review — the sharpest structural criticism**, because it comes from an *appreciative* reader: *"She is wise enough to point out that not every strategy will work for every person… **There is a chapter on changing the world and its systems, but the discussion felt too vague and generalized.**"* `[INFERENCE]` Her structural chapter — the one that answers the structural critics — is the one a sympathetic reader found least delivered. That is a real finding.

### 6.2 On the "ADHD is a gift" critique
`[THIRD PARTY]` The ADHD community contains a live, well-organised critique of strengths-framing. **René Brooks** (Black Girl Lost Keys), who appeared *alongside* McCabe on Ologies and *alongside her on the same PESI certification course*, is one of that critique's leading voices — her ADDitude appearance is titled *"Masking ADHD symptoms to go above and beyond… unpacks the idea of ADHD as a superpower"* — https://www.understood.org/en/adhd-aha-media-kit

⚠️ `[INFERENCE]` **This is the most important structural criticism of McCabe's entire position, and it comes from inside her own coalition.** McCabe's "two sides of the same coin / flip the coin to the positive" framing (file 01 §3.7) is *precisely* what the disability-justice and neurodivergence-critical camp rejects. Brooks and McCabe are billed as guests on the same podcast, on the same paid certification course, in the same summit ecosystem — **without visible friction.** ⚠️ **No public disagreement between them was found.** Recorded as a structural observation, not a conflict.

### 6.3 ⚠️ Professional-adjacent caution about the RSD material
`[THIRD PARTY, clinical]` **Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria is not a recognised diagnosis.** The TotallyADD page she appears on states this plainly: *"Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria is not an official term. Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria is a term specific to Dr. William Dodson, so if you mention it to your psychologist or psychiatrist they may not understand what you mean."* — https://totallyadd.com/add_video/answering-questions-about-adhd/
Corroborated clinically: *"Because rejection sensitive dysphoria isn't an officially recognized medical condition, there aren't any medications that are specifically approved to treat it."* — Dr. Gallagher, Cleveland Clinic, via SELF — https://www.self.com/story/rejection-sensitivity-dysphoria

⚠️ `[INFERENCE]` **RSD is her highest-virality content and her largest unflagged exposure.** She has made it enormously mainstream. `[INFERENCE]` She does credit Dodson when asked directly (via TotallyADD) — that is correct practice — but the mass of her reach is to audiences who never see the disclaimer. ⚠️ **No clinician was found publicly criticising her specifically over RSD.** The criticism exists in the literature and in clinician silence.

### 6.4 The unsourced statistic
⚠️ **"ADHD'ers are 300% more likely to start their own business."** — stated flatly, on stage, with no source — https://pickscribe.com/pl/v/JiwZQNYlGQI
`[THIRD PARTY]` A variant is echoed by Jason Wiklund (Syracuse University) in softer, properly-hedged form: *"We can see that people with ADHD are more likely to actually go into entrepreneurship…"* — https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/she-turned-adhd-into-a-career-talking-about-our-challenges-is-very-important-133257258.html
