## 1. ⚠️ STOPPING ADHD MEDICATION — the decision she has publicly called a mistake

### 1.1 The primary source, in her own words (via the TotallyADD collaboration)

`[THIRD PARTY]` TotallyADD's transcript page — *"How To Spot The Moment You're Ready To Stop Taking ADHD Meds"*:
> "**How to ADHD creator Jessica McCabe stopped taking ADHD meds because her really smart boyfriend told her they were bad for her (you should ALWAYS talk to a doctor first).** Jessica gained **30 pounds in 3 months**, lost a job, and made bad decisions. Eventually Jessica realized that the **side effects of her untreated ADHD were so much worse than the side effects of the medication** she was on and went back on her medication. She says: **'I get up, I put glasses on, I get up and take my meds, this works for me.'**"
> — https://totallyadd.com/add_video/answering-questions-about-adhd/

⚠️ `[INFERENCE]` **Note the origin of the decision: a boyfriend's opinion, not a clinical assessment.** And note that the same partner ("Edward") is the one who talked her out of naming the channel "How Not To ADHD" (https://pickscribe.com/pl/v/JiwZQNYlGQI). `[HERSELF]` She has since reframed the medication as simply *good* — the "putting on glasses" simile is doing heavy lifting and appears to have lost its ambivalence.

### 1.2 The short form and her retrospective verdict

`[THIRD PARTY]` ADDitude:
> "As an adult, Jessica decided she no longer needed medication, and stopped for about a year and a half. **'That was a bad idea,' she says.** Changing to a different medication helped, but she still struggled with ADHD symptoms. '**As I hit my early 30s, still waiting tables and struggling with my acting career, and after many failed relationships, I decided meds were not enough.**'"
> — https://www.additudemag.com/adhd-personal-stories-real-life-people-living-with-adhd/

⚠️ **⚠️ CONFLICTING DURATION AND STAKES — do not reconcile.** TotallyADD says the off-medication period was marked by +30 lb in 3 months, job loss and "bad decisions," i.e. **a fast, catastrophic collapse**. ADDitude says "about a year and a half." `[INFERENCE]` These may describe different things (how long she was off meds vs. how long the consequences took to fully play out), or different recollections. **Record both.** `[INFERENCE]` The TotallyADD version is the more vivid and is the one most likely to be quoted at her.

### 1.3 The 2017 med-stigma confrontation — the opposite decision, publicly

`[HERSELF]` The letter, "What I Want to Say to My Mom, Who 'Drugged' Me" (2017). Her stated decision logic:
> "**And was it enough? No, like I needed other tools. But you did what you could.** You do what you did what you knew to do."
> — https://sgutranscripts.org/wiki/SGU_Episode_962

`[THERSELF, via TotallyADD]` On her own decision to speak publicly about meds:
> "**Jessica McCabe found out she had ADHD when she was 12 and didn't consider what could happen if she told people about her ADHD.** Her cool aunt had ADHD and she thought it was like being part of a cool club. Jessica still had shame about things she struggled with, like not being able to keep her car clean… but **she never felt like she shouldn't talk about it. Jessica was surprised when she started talking about it on the How To ADHD YouTube channel that people were calling her brave.**"
> — https://totallyadd.com/add_video/answering-questions-about-adhd/

⚠️ `[INFERENCE]` ⚠️ **Direct contradiction with Rick Green, in the same published series.** On the same page, Green says: *"**You want to consider the risks in declaring you have ADHD. Once you have said it you can't un-say it.**"* and *"**disclosing you have ADHD is a risky move and not something he recommends.**"* `[INFERENCE]` **The two collaborators give opposite advice on disclosure risk, on the same page, and she reportedly never experienced the risk he warns about.** Her own account of going public is that the risk was one she *anticipated* and then found other people overestimated.

### 1.4 Where she landed

`[HERSELF]` **On medication now:** *"I get up, I put glasses on, I get up and take my meds, this works for me."* — https://totallyadd.com/add_video/answering-questions-about-adhd/

`[THIRD PARTY]` Medication name (unverified, single source, secondary): *"She has posted on her website that she is currently taking **Vyvanse**, and in the past has taken other medications."* — https://totallyadd.com/how-to-adhd-and-totallyadd-together/

`[HERSELF]` **And the residual she never resolves:** *"I still had a lot of the same struggles **because pills don't teach skills, it turns out.**"* — https://sgutranscripts.org/wiki/SGU_Episode_962
`[HERSELF]` Her own account of what meds did and didn't do, aged 12: *"It worked really well for me. I was one of the lucky ones where the first medication I tried right off the bat was effective for me… **But the meds didn't tell me what to focus on or, like, when things were due or how to plan or prioritize or sustain effort toward long-term goals.**"*
`[HERSELF]` Her clinical diagnosis of her own childhood treatment: *"**I had a half treatment. That's exactly what it is.** Because we know from the MTA study that multimodal treatment is what's needed for ADHD, for long-term positive outcomes. I was a really good case study on what happens when you give a kid medication and nothing else."*

⚠️ `[INFERENCE]` **The decision chain is coherent and, unusually, she tells it against her own earlier self.** Stopping was wrong → but stopping was *rational at the time* → because medication had been oversold to her as sufficient → which it was, by her own doctors' framing → so the real error was a **half-treatment**, not the meds. `[INFERENCE]` This is a genuinely good piece of reasoning and one of the strongest things in the corpus. It is also the one place where she does sustained multi-step causal reasoning *about her own medical history* — relevant to file 03.

## 2. THE TURNING POINT — "do nothing"

⚠️ **⚠️ The task brief's version of this is not supported by the source.** The brief states a life coach said *"your agent says you need to be smaller — I say you need to be bigger."*

**Business Insider's actual text** (Jan 2024) — https://www.businessinsider.com/how-jessica-mccabe-used-adhd-strengths-build-dream-career-2024-1:
> "She was trying to lose weight for a TV acting gig at the time, which led her to a life coach for actors. She came away from the meeting with a key piece of advice: **do nothing.** 'Everybody had been just telling me, "do more, catch up, make up for lost time,"' she said. '**And so this was the first time in my life where I just stopped doing anything extra. I went to work, I came home, I ate — that was it.**'"
> **"Taking the space cleared her mind enough to remember her ADHD diagnosis — and she started to question if it played a significant role in her life."**

`[THIRD PARTY]` The trigger, restated at the time: *"My brain kind of went on strike and went, 'I am not going to keep working under these conditions.'"* — same

⚠️ `[INFERENCE]` **The BI version is about subtraction from an over-loaded life, not about physical scale.** The "bigger/smaller" phrasing may come from a different, later retelling, or may be a conflation with the weight-loss context. **Use the BI version.** The structural insight — that the *first* useful thing she did was stop doing things, not start doing other things — survives either way and is corroborated by the TEDx:

`[HERSELF]` TEDx version of the same insight, generalised: *"At this point, I could have given up on myself… **But I didn't, because I knew that it was my behavior that had gotten me here, and behavior is affected by the brain, and my brain has ADHD.**"*

## 3. DECIDING TO GO PUBLIC — and to name the diagnosis she already had

`[HERSELF]` **She had the diagnosis for 20 years and did not act on it.**
> "So I decided at 32 to start really looking into this thing that I'd been diagnosed with… **So while I was diagnosed at 12, I don't think I really understood my ADHD or how to work with my brain, not against it, until I started my channel.**"
> — https://www.understood.org/en/podcasts/adhd-aha/jessica-mccabe-motherhood-social-anxiety-adhd-medication

`[HERSELF]` **The stated fear, and what happened instead:**
> "**I was really glad to see that it was almost immediately helpful for other people with ADHD, but I just really hoped that nobody who actually knew what they were doing would find out what I was doing and tell me to take it down. It was a good six, eight months before I realized that it wasn't just people with ADHD that were sharing my videos or finding them helpful. It was experts as well.**"
> — quoted via https://www.thetilt.com/content-entrepreneur/jessica-mccabe-how-to-adhd-content-business (Business Insider original)

`[HERSELF]` **Her decision rule for going public at all:**
> "I made the videos **public in case they were helpful for anybody else**, which it turns out they were."
> — https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=177809

⚠️ `[INFERENCE]` **The decision was structurally low-risk and she knew it.** She had no reputation in the domain; the worst case was "a nobody was wrong on the internet." The risk she *actually* incurred was different and unnamed: the ADHD community became her entire social world, which she later described as painful (*"It was painful moving into this parasocial space"* — https://apexnews-sea.com/en/news/jessica-mccabe-built-how-to-adhd-by-solving-her-own-biggest-problem-2931b99f260628en_zz).

## 4. THE CHANNEL NAMING DECISION

`[HERSELF]` **From the TEDx script:**
> "I had no idea how to start a YouTube channel, but I started a YouTube channel. **I almost called it 'How Not To ADHD,' because that was about all I knew at the time. But my boyfriend, Edward, talked me out of it.** It turns out lots of people need help understanding ADHD, including, maybe especially, those who actually have it. I was no exception."

⚠️ `[INFERENCE]` **The second decision she attributes to Edward is more consequential than the first**, and she undersells it: her stated premise for renaming was *who the audience is*, which is the entire strategic reorientation of her project from "here is what I know" to "here is what all of us need." `[HERSELF]` She immediately applies the new frame to herself: *"I was no exception."*

⚠️ **⚠️ But it was also the decision that created the positivity liability.** `[INFERENCE]` "How To ADHD" is an imperative that presupposes ADHD is a *solvable* skill set. Every critique in file 04 that traces back to over-promising is downstream of this one naming choice, which was made for her by someone else.

## 5. FROM ACTOR TO CONTENT CREATOR — the stated logic

`[HERSELF]` **Why acting stopped (Burnout, not failure):**
> "I hit burnout and was like, '**I can't give more. I don't know how I'm going to get to this place that everybody expects me to be at if what I'm already doing isn't working because I'm exhausted. I can't even keep this up.**'"
> — https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=177809

`[HERSELF]` **The decisive reframe — effort redirected from output to diagnosis:**
> "Maybe instead of trying to put so much effort into trying to be successful and get ahead and reach my potential, **maybe I could put that amount of effort into figuring out what was getting in my way** and what to do about it, and maybe that would make a difference."
> — same

`[THIRD PARTY]` **Why YouTube specifically could carry her ADHD (and previous jobs couldn't):**
> "McCabe said that the flexibility of YouTubing helped her lean into her talents. '**I was able to solve problems in really kind of unique, outside-the-box ways that I didn't have the freedom to do before.**'"
> — https://www.businessinsider.com/how-jessica-mccabe-used-adhd-strengths-build-dream-career-2024-1

`[THIRD PARTY]` **The joke that carries the real content:**
> "She once volunteered at a yoga studio in exchange for free classes but got caught Googling a lot on the computer. '**Now, I get to Google things for a living,**' she said. 'This kind of insatiable curiosity that I attribute to my ADHD, that's actually served me really well.'"
> — same

`[HERSELF]` **The hit she was always hoping for:** *"That's the first time I think I've actually been very glad that I struggled with all of these things."* — same

⚠️ `[INFERENCE]` **Her pivot logic is textbook interest-based matching** — the TEDx makes it explicit: *"ADHD brains are great at tackling tasks that are urgent, working with ideas that are new, wrestling with problems that are challenging, and dedicating themselves to projects that are of personal interest. **This YouTube career I'd stumbled into was all of those things.**"* (https://pickscribe.com/pl/v/JiwZQNYlGQI). `[INFERENCE]` **She never says she chose to leave acting.** She says she ran out of executive function, then describes a career that fits her traits. The decision appears to have been *received* rather than *made* — which is itself consistent with the ADHD she describes, and is worth carrying as a caution against the "she reinvented herself" narrative.

## 6. BUSINESS DECISIONS

### 6.1 ⚠️ The most credibility-protective decision: declining pharmaceutical partnerships
`[HERSELF]`, reported:
> "McCabe says that her advocacy has drawn interest from pharmaceutical firms but that she has **turned down offers of financial partnerships, explaining: 'I need to be unbiased, because I'm there to support people.'**"
> — https://www.washingtonpost.com/health/adhd-stigma-medication-inclusion/2021/08/20/a85b87f2-f52b-11eb-a49b-d96f2dac0942_story.html

⚠️ **⚠️ TENSION WITH 6.5 BELOW.** She declined pharma while accepting two paid PESI CME courses worth ~$1,939 in list price, a commercial book deal, a Patreon, and merchandise. `[INFERENCE]` **No conflict of interest has been alleged by anyone, and pharma ≠ clinical-education vendor.** But the *unbiased* claim is stronger than her other revenue streams require it to be, and it is load-bearing for her credibility. Recorded as a live tension, not a contradiction.

### 6.2 Patreon — the decision that made it a job
`[THIRD PARTY, from The Tilt]`:
> "How To ADHD became a for-profit business when Jessica launched on Patreon **because she couldn't keep producing content and maintain her waitressing job.**"
> — https://www.thetilt.com/content-entrepreneur/jessica-mccabe-how-to-adhd-content-business

Structure at that time: tiers **$2/month ("Brain Buds") to $25/month ("WonderBrains")**; benefits included exclusive content, a newsletter, Discord access, **a dedicated Minecraft server**, and **paid-member voting on what gets made first**. Patreon reported **$14.5K/month gross** to the NYT in 2021; by Aug 2021 **3,223 subscribers / >$16,000/month** via spokesperson **Linnea Toney**. — same + WaPo.

`[THIRD PARTY]` Channel scale arc: **>532,000 subs and >$15,700/mo Patreon as of Dec 2020** (https://en.wikipedia-on-ipfs.org/wiki/Jessica_McCabe) → **nearly 901,000 subs as of April 2021** (⚠️ low-quality mirror, https://www.adhd-symptoms.com/adhd-blog/jessica-mccabe — do not rely on) → **>1M by 23 Dec 2021**, celebrated with a **"1 Million Subscriber How to ADHD Q&A Livestream Special!"** → **1.6M by Jan 2024** (https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=177809) → **1.65M at time of The Tilt profile** → **1.94M at VidCon 2026** → **2M as of Oct 2026** (https://www.additudemag.com/webinar/adhd-awareness-month-2026-jessica-mccabe/).

### 6.3 Hiring the researchers — and the negotiation that produced the channel's identity
`[HERSELF]` **How the researchers arrived:**
> "So I started working with researchers. And even then, it took a little bit. **The first researcher that I worked with was very pedantic and wanted to use very technical language. And I was like, I'm trying to disseminate to a lay public. I need to simplify a bit. I need to do that without losing important nuance, but I do need to be able to restate it in words that anybody can understand.**"
> — https://apexnews-sea.com/en/news/jessica-mccabe-built-how-to-adhd-by-solving-her-own-biggest-problem-2931b99f260628en_zz

`[THIRD PARTY, earlier version]` **How it started:**
> "**Rachelle LeDuc-Cairns, a registered nurse in Canada, offered to teach her how to analyze research studies for their validity. Then Patrick LaCount, a postdoctoral fellow at the Center for Child Health, Behavior and Development at Seattle Children's Research Institute, began to meet with her weekly** to review and discuss research studies."
> — https://www.denverpost.com/2021/03/20/hello-brains-youtube-adhd/

⚠️ `[INFERENCE]` **This is a genuinely notable decision with a bad first outcome.** She asked for research help and initially got *less* accessible material, not more. The fix was to hire someone whose job was to hold the line on nuance — and the current structure (LaCount as "chief science officer," reviewing everything pre-publication) is the result of learning that lesson. `[HERSELF]` Her stated governing principle: *"Because if it's not accurate, **then what am I doing?**"* — same 2026 source.
⚠️ **`[INFERENCE]` ⚠️ The "reviews everything" claim and the "We’re on the Health Shelf" badge are `[HERSELF]`-only and independently unverified.** Contradicts the 2021 NYT text (*"her videos are not reviewed by a professional on a weekly basis"*).

### 6.4 Building the team — what she kept and what she gave up
`[HERSELF, via The Tilt, describing her own split of work:`
> "Someone handles the day-to-day operations, all the business side of things, as well as the website creation. **A volunteer community manager for the Discord channel was turned into a paid position.** Also on the team is a lead producer who works on the videos and oversees several freelance editors. **Jessica relies on a research consultant, a psychologist by training, when she needs help.**"
> — https://www.thetilt.com/content-entrepreneur/jessica-mccabe-how-to-adhd-content-business

`[HERSELF]` **Her stated ambition for the team:**
> "**I want to bring other voices on [the videos] because I think it's time. People have heard a lot of what I have to say. I think we need to hear more from other people with different experiences than mine.**"
> — same (via https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=177809)

⚠️ `[INFERENCE]` **This is the most consultative decision in her business and it deserves more attention than it gets.** She identified that she had become the bottleneck *as a narrator* and that her own experience was no longer sufficient coverage, and acted on it. ⚠️ `[THIRD PARTY]` `[THIRD PARTY]` Whether she has actually followed through is unclear — ADDitude still frames her as the sole "ADHD Expert" speaker in Oct 2026 — https://www.additudemag.com/webinar/adhd-awareness-month-2026-jessica-mccabe-/

### 6.5 ⚠️ The book deal — and the decision to hire help
`[HERSELF, ADDitude]` **Why this book, this time:**
> "It's been my dream since I was, like, 12 years. **I wanted to be a published author by the age of 15. I'm a little late to that party**, but I am a published author. **But now I really want this to be a bestseller**, and pre-orders really help with that."

`[HERSELF]` **Her four process strategies** (full text in file 01 §6): work backward; schedule four weeks of *not working on the book* to survive hyperfocus; per-chapter accountability with her editor; stay in constant communication about being late.

`[HERSELF]` **The decision that matters most:**
> "When I first started, I was really stubborn… And a couple of weeks in, I woke up one day and I went, **I need Teresa.**… **I am being ridiculous.** I wanted to do it on my own. Why? **Like, what am I trying to prove? That people with ADHD shouldn't ever need help or ask for help?** … in the end, this was not an accomplishment of 'I did it all by myself,' but also it's done and it's good. **And I don't think it would have been both of those things if I had tried to do it all by myself.**"

### 6.6 Going into professional continuing education — the paid-credential decision

Barkley also speaks; Barkley is a certification *advisor*. **⚠️ This is what Wikipedia wrongly reports as a "collaboration with Barkley."** — https://www.pesi.com/speaker/jessica-mccabe-1912676

`[INFERENCE]` **This is a deliberate, meaningful upgrade of her positioning.** She went from "lay person making YouTube videos" to "credentialed continuing-education speaker for clinicians" — moving her into the room she said she had no business being in. It is also, unavoidably, a revenue stream, and `[INFERENCE]` **it is the decision that most directly resolves her own stated imposter syndrome ("I have no business talking about this except for the fact that I have ADHD") by buying the right to be in the room.**

### 6.8 Introducing 1:1 paid coaching — reversing the parasocial boundary
`[HERSELF]`:
> "One of the things we're doing now that I'm really excited about is that **I'm going to start coaching people one-on-one. I can get the one-on-one I really value, but then we put that content online so other people can benefit, too.**"
> — same
⚠️ `[INFERENCE]` **This is the most sophisticated business decision in the file.** It solves the parasocial-overload problem she named (*"I can no longer respond to people"*) by converting it into a paid, bounded, self-funding service that **still generates public content**. It also, `[INFERENCE]`, monetises the exact thing she said she had to give up — which is either an elegant solution or the exact boundary violation she was trying to avoid. `[THIRD PARTY]` **No pricing, launch date or uptake data was found.** ⚠️ Unverified whether it launched.
