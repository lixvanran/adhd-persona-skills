## 1. The Forbes 2023 interview — Maya Richard-Craven (full working transcript)

**Source:** Forbes, 2023-09-28 · https://www.forbes.com/sites/mayarichard-craven/2023/09/28/jetblue-founder-david-neeleman-opens-up-about-having-adhd/

**Format:** short-form email/async Q&A, not a conversation. 9 questions. Total body text ≈ 700 words. This matters — see §6.

### 1.1 Journalistic frame (Tier A, but this is the *writer's* voice, not his)

> "David Neeleman isn't your average CEO. He founded several airlines and is on a mission to give back to underserved communities. The JetBlue founder and CEO of Azul Brazilian Airlines and Breeze Airways gives his entire salary away every year to educational programs and non-profits. **As a Mormon he believes in dedicating his life to being of service, which gives him a deeper sense of purpose.** The business mogul believes finding meaning in life is incredibly important. In 2002 he was diagnosed with ADHD. He believes talking about ADHD openly is a way to help others."
> — Maya Richard-Craven, Forbes

`[INFERENCE]` Note that the religious framing is **the journalist's**, delivered in the writer's voice before he speaks. His own religious language in the answers themselves is limited to the word "purpose." **Do not let the persona inherit the journalist's piety as if it were his.**

### 1.2 Verbatim Q&A

**MRC:** How long have you been in the airline industry?
**DN:** Since I was 24-year-old, so 40 years.

`[INFERENCE]` ⚠️ **He does the math in his head and it doesn't land.** Sept 2023, born Oct 1959 → he was 63, and 63−24 = 39, not 40. He answered "40 years" anyway. **This is a genuine tic, not a transcription error** — it recurs in the CAPA 2026 transcript where his non-sequitur answers run off the end of the question. Characteristic: *he rounds, he approximates, he doesn't audit.*

**MRC:** Did you have to build your connections yourself?
**DN:** I didn't come from money. I did a mission in so I built connections. I started a little business in college and it kind of, you know, blossomed and then it kind of went out because now online I was using went out of business. And then I was winter Morris travel and start helping, you know, found more Sarah together with Jim watch.

> ⚠️ **This answer is substantially garbled** — "now online I was using went out of business," "I was winter Morris travel," "found more Sarah together with Jim watch." This is almost certainly an AI-generated or ASR-mangled transcript in the search extraction, possibly also degraded in the source. **Morris Travel and June Morris are almost certainly the real referents.** But I will not silently "fix" it: **as extracted, this answer is unintelligible, and I flag that the Forbes Q&A transcript available to me is of uncertain fidelity.** Anyone quoting it should be aware. Treat the *clean* answers as reliable and this one as unusable.

**MRC:** Do you feel like ADHD has held you back in any way?
**DN:** When I finally figured out I had it, it was a relief. I was just really careful to surround myself with people that could complement my ADHD. I have people around me that help implement a lot of the ideas I have.

**MRC:** What's the most important thing you've learned from having ADHD?
**DN:** The most important thing is that if you have to follow your passion. You have to figure out what your passion is. And then once you figure it out you'll become the best in the world at it, because you're obsessed with it and don't do it but other people want you to do it. What really gets you up in the morning and gets you excited?

**MRC:** Are you so public about your ADHD?
**DN:** Because I think it's really important because. I want people to understand that it's a special gift, that it's not something that can hold you back.

> `[INFERENCE]` "because I think it's really important because." — the false start and the dangling conjunction are preserved here too. Combined with the garbled answer above, this suggests **the Forbes transcript may itself be lightly auto-transcribed or lightly edited.** Flagging rather than assuming.

**MRC:** Did you ever see yourself living this life?
**DN:** No. I live a pretty simple life. I'm not an extravagant person.

**MRC:** How do you manage your time?
**DN:** You just have to create checklists and stuff. The hard thing is the mundane stuff. The stuff that is most important, you have no problem getting them.

**MRC:** What is the most important thing you have learned from being a CEO?
**DN:** Guide your people and take care of them. Let them succeed.

**MRC:** Is there anything else you'd like to tell the readers?
**DN:** I think what's more important is to find meaning and purpose in your life. I think that's what we really lack today. I think people are struggling to find meaning and purpose. Help people. Lift people up. Until you do that, you're never going to be happy.

### 1.3 What this interview does and does not give you

- ✅ Gives: his **closing thesis** (purpose/service), his **practical ADHD method** (surround yourself with complements; checklists), his **unambition** (simple life, not extravagant), his **phrase** "special gift."
- ❌ Does **not** give: any business history, any decision reasoning, any pushback, any humor, any religion in his own voice, any adversity narrative, any concreteness.
- ❌ **It is a closing-argument interview, not an exploratory one.** The journalist got an aphorism bundle. `[INFERENCE]` Forbes 2023 is, on its own, a **poor** source for expression DNA — it yields his *content* but almost none of his *texture*.

## 5. The CAPA 2026 transcript — a pattern in his answers

Source: https://centreforaviation.com/analysis/video/breeze-airways-chairman--ceo-david-neeleman-at-the-capa-airline-leader-summit-americas-2026-2428

**His strongest recent quotable line** (about market selection):
> "**The coolest thing about this middle spot is that if we can find a market that has only 10 PPPD [per passenger per day] each way, we can make it work. There are about 3,500 of those markets, and today we're only in about 280, so there is tremendous growth in that area.**"

Also:
> "We're getting 16 new A220s this year. **Every time we put one into service, we expand the map of where we can fly nonstop.**"

**`[INFERENCE]` — the notable finding here is a NON-answer, and it's consistent across every panel/transcript I read:**
The panelist asked him directly about **fuel cost pass-through** — a quantitative, operator-level question. His answer describes *direction of travel* and *sentiment* ("it's looking really good," "we couldn't have even imagined we could come close to even covering the fuel increase"), names a statistic ("about 80% of our 324 announced routes, we're the only airline that flies them"), and then **never states a number, a margin, or a mechanism.** The transcript shows him running past the end of the question ("Are they to 2,500? You know, it's for the markets that we fly to…"; "The plane is because the, the way we understand it…").

**This is a real, repeatable pattern:** he is **fluent, warm, quotable and specific about customers, brand, markets and people**, and **vague about mechanisms, units and financials — especially under questioning.** It is not evasion-with-aggression; it is enthusiasm outrunning the question. `03-expression-dna.md` should carry this as a signature.

## 6. Format observations that belong to the expression record

1. **He does better live than in writing, and better in a group than alone.** His most fluent passages are panels, summits, and podcasts with a host who supplies narrative beats.
2. **He is a listener who finishes other people's sentences and then changes the subject back to himself.** Visible in Forbes 2023 ("What really gets you up in the morning and gets you excited?" appended to his own answer) and in the Advice Line 2026 segments.
3. **He rarely says no.** No located instance of a sustained refusal or boundary-setting.
4. **He does not argue.** In the entire corpus I found exactly **one** genuinely confrontational exchange (BuzzFeed, COVID, 2020) and even there his register is flatly dismissive rather than defensive: "There is no there there. Period." See `04-external-views.md` §1.
5. **He self-deprecates about scale but not about judgment.** He is happy to joke about 39 grandchildren or a personal net worth collapse; he does not joke about being wrong about a forecast.

## 8. Bottom line for the pipeline

- **For voice/tone/rhythm:** **go.** Forty-plus distinct quoted passages across 12+ independent sources spanning 2003–2026. Clean.
- **For decision-making voice:** **go, with care** — he narrates decisions in outcome terms, rarely in reasoning terms, and most of the reasoning is retrospective rationalization. See `05-decisions.md`.
- **For intellectual/worldview breadth:** **stop.** Do not extrapolate. He does not speak publicly about anything except aviation, service, ADHD, family, and purpose.
- **For the ADHD persona specifically:** **use the older sources first** (CBS 2003, ADDitude, Winthrop repub), and use Forbes 2023 last. Reversing that order produces a flat, sanctimonious persona.
