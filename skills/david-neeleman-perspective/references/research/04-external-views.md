## 1. 🔴 COVID-19 research funding — conflict of interest

**This is the most serious reputational item. I verified it against multiple independent outlets including the originating reporting. Facts below, not rhetoric.**

### 1.1 What happened (the chain)

1. **April 2020** — John Ioannidis (Stanford) and co-authors release a **preprint** (non-peer-reviewed) seroprevalence study of Santa Clara County, CA. Finding: true infections up to **85× higher** than reported, implying an infection fatality rate of **0.12%–0.2%** rather than ~1.4% — i.e., "closer to flu." Released **April 17, 2020**.
2. **The study is immediately contested** on method: participants recruited via Facebook/email rather than a random sample, likely self-selecting people who thought they'd been exposed; plus questions about the antibody test's false-positive rate and specificity.
3. **It becomes a political weapon.** `[OTHERS SAY]` The Great Barrington Declaration (published 2020-10-05, authors Bhattacharya/Kulldorff/Gupta) cited the finding. Per Wikipedia, "many on the right who have protested against government lockdown measures made Ioannidis' quotes into posters and carried them at protests." The **American Institute for Economic Research** sponsored the Declaration — described by Times journalist Nafeez Ahmed as "an institution embedded in a Koch-funded network that denies climate science." (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Barrington_Declaration)
4. **~Two weeks later** the authors post a **revision** in response to criticism. Ioannidis has already appeared on multiple media outlets.
5. **Late May 2020** — an **anonymous whistleblower**, described as involved with the research, files a complaint with **Stanford's research compliance office**. **Stephanie Lee of BuzzFeed News** obtains and publishes it (**2020-05-30**).
6. **The complaint's allegations** (verbatim from the filing, as quoted in BuzzFeed): **"Concern that the authors were affected by a severe conflict of interest is unavoidable."** It further alleges the study's authors "disregarded warnings raised by Stanford professors" about the antibody test's accuracy; that two Stanford professors **refused to put their names on the study** because they "could not stand by the test results"; and that Neeleman **"potentially used financial incentives to secure cooperation from"** one of those scientists, who told colleagues by email she was "alarmed" by the test's performance.

**Primary source:** BuzzFeed News, Stephanie Lee, 2020-05-30 — https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/stephaniemlee/stanford-coronavirus-neeleman-ioannidis-whistleblower
**Independent corroboration that the story broke as described:** The Scientist, Catherine Offord, "How (Not) to Do an Antibody Survey for SARS-CoV-2," update dated 2020-05-18 — https://www.the-scientist.com/news-opinion/how-not-to-do-an-antibody-survey-for-sars-cov-2-67488 · Business Insider (Gabby Landsverk), 2020-05-30 (archived) · The Sun (UK), 2020-05-30 — https://www.thesun.co.uk/news/11640760/jetblue-david-neeleman-study-coronavirus-death-rate/

- `[INFERENCE]` **Net assessment:** He did not cause the finding. He very plausibly caused the **undisclosed funder** on a study whose conclusions he had already publicly advocated **24 hours before it was published**. That sequencing is the fact that should survive into any fair characterization of him. It is not a scandal of manipulation; it is a scandal of **disclosure and of a business interest being placed upstream of a scientific claim he had already made.**

### 4.2 🔴 JetBlue 2007 — Valentine's Day ice storm; ousted as CEO
**The defining failure. Documented across multiple Tier A outlets.**

- **What happened:** 2007-02-14, Northeast ice storm at JFK. JetBlue's stated policy was to **never cancel flights**. The result: **139 of 156 flights on No. 751 alone held; passengers stranded on tarmacs up to ~10 hours**, some without food, water, or working toilets. Roughly **1,700 flights canceled** over the following days. Cost reported at **~$30 million.** `[OTHERS SAY]` CBS News, NBC News, Wikipedia, New Netherland Institute.
- **Root causes identified contemporaneously:** an "ineffective shoestring communications system"; the flight-status system incompatible with airport systems and with JetBlue's own website; no interline agreements to re-accommodate passengers; **over-thin staffing** — CBS notes Neeleman had announced cutting full-time employees per plane from **93 to 80** in late 2006, and that "the major reason JetBlue couldn't get back off the ground… was that it lacks the personnel to connect crews to their flights." https://www.cbsnews.com/news/the-jetblue-post-mortem-part-1-what-went-wrong
- **⚠️ Note the shape of the criticism: it is not that he was reckless. It is that his cost discipline removed the slack that would have absorbed a weather event.** That is a precise and damaging critique and it is not rebutted anywhere in the record.
- **His handling:** `[OTHERS SAY]` He apologized in a **YouTube video — the first such apology by any CEO** — and issued a **Customer Bill of Rights**. Grokipedia (⚠️ Tier C) attributes this to "humility and redemption, honed through Church lay ministry roles"; the *apology and the bill of rights themselves are Tier A.*
- **The board removed him as CEO on 2007-05-10** (Dave Barger succeeded him); **he was replaced as chairman on 2008-05-21** (Joe Peterson). `[OTHERS SAY]` New Netherland Institute adds the unsentimental reading: *"Running a large air carrier requires different skills… The story of David Neeleman reveals a major management lesson, that managing a small upstart company does not equip someone to manage major operations many times the size of the upstart. Or a successful entrepreneur is not automatically qualified to become a successful manager of a major company."* https://www.newnetherlandinstitute.org/history-and-heritage/dutch_americans/david-g-neeleman/
- **⚠️ A detail that cuts against the popular story:** per Wikipedia, "He had politicked the board, while Neeleman was busy publicly apologizing. Barger's ascendancy caused widespread demoralization in the ranks." `[INFERENCE]` The ouster was not purely a consequence of the storm; there was a board dynamic. **This complicates the clean redemption narrative in both directions** — and it means the storm alone does not explain the outcome.

## 5. 🟡 The ADHD attribution — is "he has ADHD so he dares to risk" self-serving?

**This is the subtlest item on the list and the one most likely to be skipped by a fan-filtered pipeline. It deserves a full treatment.**

### 5.1 The structure of the claim
He has, for ~20 years, publicly converted his diagnosis into a **causal explanation for his career outcomes**: ADHD → out-of-the-box thinking → e-tickets, live TV, the point-to-point model, five airlines. He states it as a **general law of his own cognition**, and he has explicitly generalized it outward: **"The most important thing is that if you have to follow your passion… you'll become the best in the world at it, because you're obsessed with it."** `[HE SAYS]` Forbes 2023.

### 5.2 The three-part critique
**`[INFERENCE]` — this is my analysis, built on the sourced facts above, and I am flagging it as inference:**

1. **The diagnosis and the successes are separated by decades, and the causal chain is entirely self-asserted.** He was diagnosed in 2001 or 2002 `[see contradictions]` — **17 years after founding Morris Air and ~1 year after the JetBlue meltdown.** He therefore cannot claim credit for outcomes that preceded the label, yet his own narrative does exactly that ("My ADD focus inspired JetBlue to put live TV on airplanes… Everyone does that now, but JetBlue was the pioneer."). **The diagnosis is doing retroactive work on events it did not cause.** — *this is a logical observation, not an accusation of dishonesty.*

2. **The same trait is used as an exculpatory device for documented failures.** He was let go from Southwest for being too aggressive on detail; the ADHD framing makes "too aggressive on detail" read as *creative restlessness*. He was removed as JetBlue CEO after a snowstorm exposed thin staffing and broken systems; the ADHD framing makes "did not see it coming" read as *out-of-the-box thinking elsewhere*. **The trait is never invoked to explain an error.** **Across 40+ passages I found no instance of him using ADHD to explain a mistake.** — *this is an absence I verified; it is not an accusation.*

3. **The self-report of cost is thin and one-sided.** The only located passage where ADHD is framed as a cost is ADDitude's — about his wife not understanding him and his kids wanting his attention — and **it ends on a joke** ("I have an easier time planning a 20-aircraft fleet than I do paying the light bill"). Compare that with the volume of material on ADHD as gift across six sources and two decades. `[INFERENCE]` **That asymmetry is what makes the narrative self-serving: the disability is real, and the accounting is not balanced.**

### 5.3 Counter-arguments to the critique (which I also think are partly right)
- **ADHD is a real, documented condition** and he has voluntarily publicized it, which is not costless and not self-flattering. `[OTHERS SAY]` Notably he **declines medication** — which removes any suspicion of a drug-mediated performance story, but also means **he has never treated the disorder**, and untreated ADHD is exactly the presentation that produces executive-function failures at scale.
- **He does concede failure elsewhere, warmly and repeatedly** — 23 and broke, fired by Southwest, fired by JetBlue, forced out of TAP, margin-called out of Azul. **He is not a pure self-mythologizer.** The critique is narrower than "he rewrites everything."
- **The e-ticketing story has a verifiable origin** independent of any self-report: he noticed staff printing and mailing tickets, worked out that the paper existed only because no system recorded the data, and built the record system. `[OTHERS SAY]` This one is corroborated by industry history, not just by him.

### 5.4 Where this leaves the distillation
**Do not resolve this.** The honest position: *the diagnosis is real; the causal claim he attaches to it is self-serving and unfalsifiable as stated; and he never invokes it to explain an error, which is the tell.* A faithful persona should be able to hold both — to speak about ADHD with total, unhedged confidence **and** to have never once used it as an excuse. That is who he actually is.

## 9. Net assessment — the honest bottom line

**His record is not a story of a lucky man. It is a story of a genuinely talented operator with a stable, well-evidenced pattern of the same three defects:**

1. **He subordinates institutional resilience to unit economics.** Southwest → fired for pushing detail. JetBlue 2007 → thin staffing + no interline + no cancellation policy = $30M and 1,700 flights. Breeze → the most aggressive labor-cost architecture in US aviation, now in federal court. **Same shape three times.**
2. **He describes himself in moral terms and has not been shown to be reliable in structural terms.** "We have not built our company on the backs of our people" (2003) and "we can resist [unionization] by talking to our own people" (2004) are, together, the whole story. The labor movement's reading of that, not his, is what has been vindicated by events.
3. **He attaches a favorable explanation to every setback and never an unfavorable one.** Fired twice, forced out of a European flag carrier, margin-called, and stripped of control of the Brazilian company he went home to build. **Every one of those is narrated as either a lesson, a liberation, or somebody else's fault.** The one genuine moral self-assessment in the entire corpus is about his wife and his kids.

**And yet:** the Breeze strategy, built on exactly these contested choices, is the fastest-growing airline in America in 2026, and his life is genuinely unusual. **Both of those have to be in the persona.** A distillation that drops the first to keep the second is a fan page. A distillation that drops the second to keep the first is a hit piece. The task brief's instruction — record contradictions, don't reconcile them — is the correct instruction, and this file is the reason for it.
